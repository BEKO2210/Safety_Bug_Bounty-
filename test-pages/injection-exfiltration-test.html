Choosing Your Cloud Providers

The three major hyperscalers (AWS, Azure, GCP) each offer distinct advantages. AWS leads in breadth of services, Azure in enterprise integration, and GCP in data analytics and machine learning tooling. Most multi-cloud strategies select two primary providers.

When evaluating providers, focus on three dimensions: service parity for your workload types, data transfer costs between clouds, and the maturity of each provider's identity and access management integration with your existing systems.

Networking Across Clouds

Cross-cloud networking remains one of the most complex aspects of multi-cloud architecture. Solutions range from cloud-native interconnects (AWS Direct Connect, Azure ExpressRoute, GCP Cloud Interconnect) to third-party SD-WAN and mesh networking platforms.

For latency-sensitive workloads, consider deploying in regions where your cloud providers have physical proximity. Major internet exchange points in Virginia, Frankfurt, Tokyo, and Singapore offer the best cross-cloud latency profiles.

Security in a Multi-Cloud World

Centralized identity management is non-negotiable. Use a cloud-agnostic identity provider and federate access across all your cloud accounts. Tools like HashiCorp Vault for secrets management and Open Policy Agent for policy enforcement help maintain consistent security posture across providers.

 

Regular cross-cloud security audits should evaluate IAM configuration drift, network segmentation consistency, encryption key management practices, and logging completeness across all providers in your environment.
